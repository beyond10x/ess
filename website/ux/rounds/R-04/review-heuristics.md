## The two site changes

### A. The sticky sidebar rule (`website/src/css/custom.css:8-26`)

Verdict: it does what it claims at the gated states; one residue and one unchecked edge.

- Claim: at 997 px and wider the sidebar column is as tall as its links (up to 100vh) and a doc page
  is at least `100vh + 10rem` tall (a blog row with a sidebar `100vh + 16rem`), so a sticky sidebar
  has room to stay in view halfway down a short page.
- Measured: `gates.gate2_always_visible` pages 2 to 0, failures 4 to 0 (R-03 to R-04);
  `/ess/docs/getting-started@desktop-1280/{light,dark}` and `/ess/releases/tags@desktop-1280/{light,dark}`
  all `always_visible.failures` 0 (`round.json`, scenarios). At the measured-only widths desktop-1024
  went 2 to 0 pages with an element out of view and tablet-768 stays 0.
- Not covering content: gate 1 passes (0 spills, 0 page scroll, 139 of 139); gate 3 passes
  (`completion_miss` 0, `false_complete` 0, 22 of 22 runs); the sidebar sits in its own column, left
  of the article, in `contact-light.png` / `contact-dark.png` (`docs-intro`, `install`,
  `cli-middle`), with the article and the contents column clear of it. `cli-middle` shows the
  sidebar in place, current section open, with the page scrolled.
- Residue 1: at desktop-1440 the measured-only table still reports 1 page with an element out of
  view in a gated state (R-03: 4; `report.md`, "Measured-only viewports"). `round.json` names no
  page for it, so I cannot say whether it is a sidebar. The report does not say either.
- Residue 2: the `end` state is unchanged, 136 docs and 136 blog configurations out of view
  (`gates.gate2_always_visible.measured_only`; UXF-002 and UXF-003, accepted under D15). The rule
  does not claim to fix it; at the end the sidebar still slides under the navbar.
- Unchecked edge (source reading, not observed): `max-height: 100vh` on a sidebar that starts below
  the navbar is 60 px taller than the space left under it; if the framework's own value was
  `calc(100vh - navbar)`, the bottom entries of an expanded long sidebar would sit below the
  viewport edge. Gate 2 tests that the element intersects the viewport, not that its bottom edge is
  in it. No shot has a long, expanded sidebar at 1280 x 800.
- Cost: every doc page is now at least `100vh + 10rem` tall, so a short page gains blank space and
  a scroll range it did not have, and the footer drops below the fold. That is the stated trade; no
  layout-shift metric moved with it (`layout_shift.load` sums are within the host-load noise of
  UXF-026).

### B. The index lists on `reference/spec-versions` and `reference/diagnostics`

Verdict: they work for the task they were built for and are not too long; keyboard and phone are
better than before but not good, and the diagnostics index is narrower than its name.

What they are (`website/docs/reference/spec-versions.md:11`, `diagnostics.md:10`): one paragraph,
"Jump to a version of `ess/`: " then 37 `<a href="#ess-N"><code>ess/N</code></a>` separated by
" · "; and "Jump to the class of `ESS-<FAMILY>-<NNN>`: " then 19 `<a href="#class-NNN"><code>NNN</code></a>`.
The target is an empty `<a id>` in a table cell (`| ess/31 | <a id="ess-31"></a>[0.x][rN] | ... |`,
37 on the first page, 19 on the second). Both are generated (`cargo xtask format-history`,
`diagnostics`) and carry a Markdown `generated by` marker, which is good provenance.

Pointer and phone, measured: `ess-31-release` 25 to 3 actions at desktop-1280 and 33 to 5 at
mobile-390 (card limit 6); `diagnostic-type-009/mobile-390` 7 to 4 (limit 5); both off gate 4's
over-limit list (`report.md`, Screen-only user table; `round.json`). Both `/ess/docs/reference/*`
pages: `axe.serious_critical_nodes` 0 at both viewports. Cost: first-screen reading load rose,
`ess-31-release/desktop-1280` `reading_load.max` 291 to 358 and the mobile median 117 to 137 (taken
under `R-04-worse`, `decision.md`).

Length: 37 links at 390 px wrap to about 6 lines, 19 links to about 3; in numeric order, so a
reader who knows the number finds it by position. That is scannable. The format-version index covers
only the `ess/` family: the page's other families (`ess-diff/`, `ess-conformance/` ... , from line
491 on) have no index; they are reachable through the page's contents column, which lists headings
only. The diagnostics index covers the 19 classes, not the 77 codes in the later tables; a reader
holding `ESS-SYNTH-011` has the section headings only (`diagnostics.md:184`), and the bare numbers
"001" to "019" carry no class name, so a reader who does not remember that 009 is `UNSUPPORTED` has
to open it to learn.

Keyboard (source reading; the keyboard cards are the two anchor tasks, not these): the links are
ordinary anchors, so Tab reaches them and Enter jumps and moves focus to the target, and Back
returns to the index. But the 37 links are 37 tab stops in a row, so `ess/31` is 31 presses after
the first; before that the reader has the framework's navbar and sidebar stops (the anchor cards
already take 21 to 23 presses to a sidebar link at desktop and 128 to 131 on a phone, UXF-015).
The paragraph has no heading, no landmark and no label of its own, so a screen reader reads it as one
long paragraph and may read each " · " aloud. No skip over the list.

Phone (shots show no reference page; source and widths only): inline links 13 to 16 px high in a
wrapped paragraph, separated by a dot, are exempt from WCAG 2.5.8 as inline links but are close
together; a mis-tap lands on a neighbouring version. Not observed.

After the jump (not observed, source reading): the target is an `<a id>` in the second cell. Custom
CSS has no `scroll-margin-top` for it and no `:target` style, so the row may land under the sticky
navbar and nothing marks which row the reader jumped to among rows up to 1,459 px tall (UXF-010,
R-01). Gate 3's `completion_miss` 0 for both cards says the bot saw its target in view, so the
covered-by-navbar case did not show; no shot shows the landing.
