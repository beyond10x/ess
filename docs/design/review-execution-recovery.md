## Decision and boundaries

A finite invocation may compare desired documents, attempt a bounded ordered set of external operations and report what it can establish. It must not infer applied state from equality with a caller's previous desired document. Once an external operation may have started, a missing success record or a failed child process does not establish that nothing changed.

Recovery must preserve uncertainty at the affected release, retain known earlier outcomes, and stop later mutations when an operation or its durable recording is unresolved. A later explicit invocation observes and decides again under the same bound target/ownership policy; it does not blindly resume a remembered list of commands. This is a finite workflow, with no background watch, eventual-convergence promise, automatic endless retry or continuous drift repair. Each selected apply or removal is attempted at most once in one invocation. Helm's own behavior within that call is a separate external contract.

The supported recovery contract is conservative: when identity, authority, ownership or the required observation cannot be established, refuse state-dependent mutation and name the unresolved claim. Refusal leaves the state **unknown**, not absent, rolled back or successfully reconciled. A repair mechanism may only be admitted after its semantics are modeled; this document supplies no generic force switch that upgrades uncertainty into proof.

Per-release `--atomic` does not coordinate multiple releases. ESS issues no compensating calls for previously successful releases when a later call fails. Even the outcome of the failed release remains unproven by ESS: a nonzero Helm exit is not independent proof that a rollback completed, and a process exit of zero is not a perpetual state guarantee.

Chart-cache origin and release-attestation trust have separate owners, `story:review-cache-origin` and `story:review-delivery-trust-contract`. Recovery must not call a matching local checksum registry-origin proof or call an attached conformance log verified execution. Their future implementations may supply stronger evidence; this design does not take ownership of them or claim they have landed.

## Four different claims

These terms describe behavior and evidence obligations. They are **not** a proposed serialized enum, receipt schema, field list or lifecycle model.

| Claim | Meaning and limit |
|---|---|
| Desired | Validated intent supplied for this invocation, including exact artifact references/digests and release bindings. It says what is requested, not what exists. A prior desired document is a comparison input only. |
| Observed | A bounded statement acquired from the selected external authority at a particular observation point. It may establish present, absent, matching or differing state only for the part actually observed. The admissible source, content comparison and freshness rule are UNMAPPED. Observation cannot invent a historical execution attribution. |
| Applied | A historical claim that a particular authorized operation against the bound target completed, supported by admitted durable execution evidence. It is not equivalent to “currently matches desired.” External process success without durable evidence is only an acknowledgement available to the running process. |
| Unknown | The required claim cannot be established: missing, corrupt, stale, conflicting or wrongly scoped evidence; indeterminate process outcome; unresolved identity/ownership; or failed/insufficient observation. Unknown is neither success nor a negative assertion about the target. |

Two documents with the same desired digest do not establish either of the latter two positive claims. A chart in cache proves neither that Helm ran nor which cluster it affected. A valid release manifest proves no deployment attempt. A successful readback may establish that the target now matches, while the identity of the earlier operation remains unknown; do not manufacture an applied-history record to make those facts agree.

## Finite workflow requirements

The following ordering is binding at the behavioral level. Exact durable representation and observation acquisition remain prerequisites in the modeling section.

1. Admit the complete desired document and any comparison/baseline input. Preserve JSON/YAML unknown-field, duplicate-key and intrinsic-invariant refusals. Resolve the intended target, ownership, baseline authority and concurrency preconditions before a state-dependent mutation. `--allow-removals` authorizes a reviewed removal set; it does not prove ownership or target identity.
2. For dry-run, report an **unverified comparison preview** from admitted local inputs and stop. No external observation, ORAS/Helm/Kubernetes call, cache population or execution-evidence write occurs. A preview cannot establish a live no-op, successful application or absence. Existing argument syntax is not changed by this document.
3. For execution, obtain the observations the modeled policy requires. Equality of desired documents never substitutes for this step. A no-op requires fresh, correctly scoped evidence of the relevant desired state, not an old desired file or an old success record alone. Observation failure leaves the needed state unknown and refuses mutation dependent on it.
4. Before launching each mutation, require that its identity/authority and durable tracking can be established. Refuse before launch when that prerequisite is unavailable. Acquire/validate the chart and prepare values without treating either as target progress. Iterate desired releases in canonical rollout order, and authorized retirements afterward in reverse baseline rollout order.
5. After each external call, distinguish launch refusal, acknowledgement, definite observed outcome and uncertainty. Persist the permitted outcome before later mutations. If a child may have run but the result or its durable recording is uncertain, stop; do not issue later applies, removals or compensating rollback calls. A later local error cannot erase an earlier external effect.
6. A new explicit recovery invocation re-admits desired inputs and durable evidence, re-establishes target/ownership/concurrency authority and obtains required observations. It may acknowledge an observed match/absence without replaying the uncertain mutation, but must retain unknown historical attribution. It may execute a new authorized repair only after the observation/ownership policy establishes that action as safe; no “retry means repeat every command” rule is admitted.
7. A successful completion claim is limited to the bounded work whose required outcomes and durable recording have been established. It is not a claim that the environment will stay unchanged. An unresolved release, recording error or withheld removal prevents an overall recovered/applied-success claim. State the completed prefix and unresolved remainder rather than asserting global rollback or global success.

No all-release rollback is selected. Previously settled releases remain settled historical outcomes when a later release fails. Explicit subsequent repair can change them through a newly authorized plan; this design does not promise compensation, exactly-once external effects, or a global transaction across Helm and local storage.

## Required failure and recovery matrix

Each row supplies one observable outcome under its stated precondition and a practical offline test strategy. Rows mentioning durable evidence or observation target **future** seams, not facilities that exist today. “Stop” means fail the invocation without launching subsequent mutations; it does not mean the external state is known. For indexed rows, inject the boundary at **each** selected apply index and each removal index, preserving the earlier prefix; one first-release example is insufficient.

The future Rust fake must retain independent synthetic target state and a detailed call trace across two CLI processes. A deterministic process control must distinguish “did not launch,” “launched without effect,” “effect happened then failure/lost acknowledgement,” and success. A separate storage control must fail/read back evidence without editing the fake target. No real credentials, registry or cluster are needed. Use target/ scratch, with TMPDIR inside the assigned tree; do not add shell/Python executors.

| ID / boundary | Observable outcome required | Practical fake-executor strategy |
|---|---|---|
| R01 Invalid desired or current document | Refuse before any external call or recovery/cache write. | Retain existing JSON/YAML duplicate-key and whole-plan cases; snapshot all fake/state/storage paths before invocation. |
| R02 Unresolved/changed target, ambiguous release address or untrusted baseline | Refuse state-dependent execution before fetch/mutation; identify the unresolved authority rather than choosing one. | Give two service entries the same modeled target release, alias/remapped contexts, or an unrelated baseline; assert no effect trace. These target/ownership test identities await the modeling decision. |
| R03 Valid dry-run, including equal desired and removal preview | Emit only an unverified local preview; zero external calls and no durable execution/cache writes. | Seed a differing fake target and a call trap; snapshot cache/evidence bytes; exercise with and without current/removal flags. No live observation is performed to make the preview “fresh.” |
| R04 No usable execution evidence on a first/restarted invocation, with observation unavailable | Refuse state-dependent execution as unknown; do not infer an empty target. | Omit evidence, make the observer unavailable and retain pre-existing fake releases across process restart; assert no mutation. |
| R05 Failure preparing the fetch directory or starting ORAS | Stop before Helm; report artifact acquisition incomplete and no target mutation attempted by this operation. | Inject storage failure before process launch or fake spawn refusal; keep prior settled releases in independent fake state. |
| R06 ORAS fails/interruption during pull, including partial downloaded output | Stop before Helm; partial local output is not an admitted chart or target progress. | Fake ORAS writes a partial artifact then fails; restart with the residue and assert a complete acquisition/admission is required before any Helm call. |
| R07 ORAS succeeds, interruption before selecting/reading its archive | Stop with chart preparation unresolved and no Helm mutation for this release. | Preserve completed staged output, interrupt before archive selection; a fresh invocation must re-admit acquisition instead of treating the residue as applied evidence. |
| R08 Download succeeds but archive cardinality/read is invalid | Refuse this artifact before Helm, preserving prior target outcomes. | Fake zero/multiple `.tgz` files or an injected read error after pull; assert exact call prefix and no Helm for this release. |
| R09 Failure after writing cache archive but before checksum completes | Stop before Helm; incomplete cache state is inadmissible. | Fail each cache write boundary, then restart; assert no use of the incomplete pair as a warm hit. A new validated acquisition may complete the cache, but only after the original reference is re-admitted. |
| R10 Both cache files exist but local checksum differs/cannot be read | Refuse chart reuse before Helm; do not silently bless or overwrite inconsistent proof. | Seed corrupt payload, corrupt checksum and read-failure variants. Existing fail-closed checksum behavior is the control; explicit repair/refetch policy belongs to cache work. |
| R11 Complete consistent cache survives interruption; fresh authorized observation requires an apply | Reuse the locally consistent chart without ORAS, then perform the authorized apply; cache presence must not count as prior application. | Restart with the valid pair, modeled authority and an independently unchanged fake target needing apply; assert no ORAS and the required Helm call. This proves local reuse only, not OCI provenance. |
| R12 Values preparation/tracking fails, or interruption occurs after values write but before Helm launch | Stop before that apply; earlier settled results remain and this operation has no target mutation attempt. | Fail values serialization/write or the modeled pre-mutation storage operation separately, and cut after successful values write before launch; assert no Helm for this index and re-establish authority on restart. |
| R13 Helm apply cannot be launched | Stop; this apply did not execute, while earlier outcomes remain unchanged. | Inject spawn refusal at each apply index and inspect the ordered trace and independent target state. |
| R14 Helm apply starts, then nonzero exit, timeout or lost acknowledgement | Stop with this release's applied outcome unknown; retain settled earlier releases and leave later releases/removals untouched. | Fake both “no external change” and “changed then failure” under the same failing process outcome; assert identical uncertainty classification, no guessed rollback and no subsequent mutation. |
| R15 Helm apply returns success, interruption/failure before durable outcome is established | Stop without an overall success claim; restart must not infer the effect from missing evidence or replay automatically. | Commit fake target change first, then fail the evidence write or terminate at the handoff. Restart using the same target storage; retain the acknowledgement/evidence gap. |
| R16 Apply outcome durably recorded, interruption before the next action/final summary | Recovery reads the settled historical outcome but rechecks current state before using it to skip; no already-matching mutation is replayed merely to recreate stdout. | Stop after durable storage, discard stdout, restart and have an authoritative matching observation; assert a skip justified by that observation and continuation only of the unresolved remainder. |
| R17 Partial multi-release apply: first settled, next indeterminate, others unstarted | Report a partial result with the indeterminate release named; no global success or rollback claim and no removal phase. | Three distinct releases, fail the second after effect. Assert first evidence/state survives, second is unknown, third and all retirements have no mutation calls. Repeat at each apply index. |
| R18 Restart with corrupt, unsupported or wrongly scoped execution evidence | Refuse to use it as recovery authority; identify unknown state and leave it available for diagnosis. | Truncate/alter/version-mismatch evidence in storage separately from fake target; assert no “absent,” “success,” overwrite-to-success or automatic replay. |
| R19 Restart with valid but stale evidence, and no fresh observation | Refuse the stale no-op/application claim; leave relevant state unknown. | Advance an injected clock or observation boundary while retaining bytes; make fresh observation fail. No wall-clock sleeps or nondeterministic race is required. |
| R20 Equal desired plans after an observed manual change | Refuse a no-op claim and report observed drift requiring a newly authorized repair decision; equality must not suppress the observation. | Preserve equal input bytes, change the fake target between processes and supply a fresh differing observation. Assert no success-by-equality and no implicit overwrite of the manual change. |
| R21 Unknown earlier apply, fresh authorized observation now matches desired | Resolve the current-state question as observed matching without replay, while earlier applied attribution remains unknown. | Fake “effect then lost reply,” restart and observe exact match; assert no duplicate mutation and no invented historical applied record. |
| R22 Removal set present but `--allow-removals` absent | Refuse the invocation before applies or uninstalls. | Retain a reviewed baseline with a retirement and another desired apply; assert both are withheld, preserving current CLI ordering of this guard. |
| R23 Authorized removal cannot launch | Stop with that removal unattempted, preserving previous apply/removal outcomes; leave later removals untouched. | Inject spawn refusal at each reverse-order removal index after successful applies. |
| R24 Uninstall starts, then fails/times out/loses acknowledgement | Stop with removal outcome unknown, not “absent”; later removals stay untouched. | Fake both still-present and removed-then-failed target states for the same process failure; assert no inference from exit status or message text alone. |
| R25 Uninstall succeeds, then evidence persistence fails/interruption occurs | Stop without a durable removal-success claim; missing evidence must not trigger unconditional repeated uninstall. | Remove from fake target, fail storage, restart with retained target state and separate observation; retain unknown historical removal attribution even when fresh authorized observation establishes current absence. |
| R26 Explicit repeat removal, with authoritative fresh absence and matching ownership/target history | Complete as **observed already absent**, without another uninstall or a fabricated new removal effect. | Retain the prior removal identity context, return authoritative absence and assert zero uninstall calls. A generic Helm “not found” failure is not this observation. |
| R27 Repeat removal, but address now belongs to a different/unowned release | Refuse removal and identify the ownership conflict. | Recreate a different fake release at the old namespace/name before retry; assert `--allow-removals` cannot adopt/delete it. Ownership distinction is a modeling prerequisite. |
| R28 Concurrent or intervening actor invalidates admitted authority/observation | Stop before any mutation whose precondition is no longer protected; retain unresolved state. | Deterministically invalidate the modeled concurrency token/serialization precondition between observation and effect. No live race is needed. If no enforceable precondition exists, this workflow remains refused for that authority scope. |
| R29 Final durable recording fails after all external calls returned success | Report incomplete recovery evidence, not complete applied success; later invocation observes without assuming all calls must be repeated. | Fail finalization independently of per-release storage, then restart with matching/absent fake state and retained earlier evidence. The finalization representation itself remains UNMAPPED. |

These are design vectors, not executed test cases or a claim that 29 tests suffice. Several rows have multiple variants and every indexed boundary expands with the selected set. A future test report must identify which concrete vectors actually executed against its recorded subject.

## Modeling prerequisite: grounded inputs and UNMAPPED semantics

The following must be resolved in a separately scoped ESS modeling step before any concrete receipt/evidence fields, new persisted types or implementation decomposition. Where no authoritative code/contract supplies a relation or cardinality, record `UNMAPPED` rather than picking a plausible value. The accepted model must be validated with the repository's actual ESS tooling; a prose table is not that validation.

| Concern | Grounded input | UNMAPPED decision and what would settle it |
|---|---|---|
| Execution identity and retry identity | Exact desired digest and stable service identifiers exist; a finite CLI invocation exists. | **UNMAPPED:** whether one logical attempt spans restarts, how a retry relates to it, how collision/reuse is rejected and what survives loss of storage. Obtain the intended operation/attempt semantics from an owned executor contract. A random id or desired digest alone does not answer this. |
| Target and credential authority | `cluster` is an executor-selected kubeconfig context string; release namespace/name are strings. | **UNMAPPED:** physical target identity, context aliases and rebinding, credential provenance/scope and the authority that permits observation and mutation. Two context strings may differ while targeting one system; one string may later target another. The current string-equality guard proves neither. No credential bytes belong in execution evidence. |
| Baseline-reference authority | `--current` is another validated desired document; its digest is reproducible. | **UNMAPPED:** who may nominate that baseline, how it is associated with the target/current history, and whether it is an owned object or merely a reference. Equality of environment/cluster/digest must not be invented as sufficient proof. |
| Ownership and cardinality | A desired release map is keyed by service; rollout dependencies order those entries. | **UNMAPPED:** ownership of external releases and evidence, service-to-actual-release cardinality, cross-environment sharing, alias collision, retained-history lifetime and ownership after manual replacement. Resolve before approving deletion or declaring an `owns`/`references` relation. |
| Observation and freshness | Existing code observes child exit status and a local checksum, not target state. | **UNMAPPED:** supported observation API and its authenticated source, which desired properties it can compare, absence semantics, freshness boundary, clock/range and errors. Helm metadata alone may not establish every workload effect; choose and state the actual supported comparison. |
| Concurrent writers | Sequential loops constrain only one process. | **UNMAPPED:** whether a local exclusion mechanism is sufficient, who else may mutate the target, how an observation stays authoritative until effect, and the behavior when that protection is lost. Do not claim a local file lock excludes another workstation or a cluster controller. |
| Durable evidence authority and retention | No current delivery envelope stores execution evidence. | **UNMAPPED:** producer trust, storage owner, append/update/replace behavior, cardinality of outcomes/attempts, atomicity/durability boundary, corruption handling and retention. Persistence before and after external effects cannot make those systems one transaction. |
| Removal and retries | Removals require an explicit flag and reverse baseline order; current uninstall has no independently verified absence result. | **UNMAPPED:** deletion ownership, identity after replacement, exact supported absence proof, cleanup/finalizer guarantees and whether repeating an operation is safe. Select the contract before any idempotency claim or “not found means success” behavior. |
| Completion and compatibility | Current CLI has text output, success/failure exit and strict desired/release formats. | **UNMAPPED:** exact future result serialization, exit taxonomy, how incomplete evidence is exposed, old-client behavior and any new consumer. Give a new version to changed persisted meanings/envelopes; do not graft receipt fields onto `ess-deployment/1` or `ess-release/1`. |

The modeling step should compare bounded alternatives where evidence is absent: local single-writer versus externally serialized authority; a caller-supplied independently verified observation versus an edge-owned observation adapter; immutable attempt history versus an explicitly governed mutable recovery record. None is selected by this design. The constraints above reject unsupported claims whichever alternative is chosen.

The step must produce a validated typed home, explicit ownership/cardinality and accepted identity/authority decisions, compatibility consequences, and a mapping from every matrix precondition to its source of authority. Unsupported preconditions must yield a named refusal; they must not be filled with a fabricated successful observation. Only then can root decompose the implementation through AEP. No AEP dependency enters ESS.

## Verification and completion boundary

This unit is prose-only: source inspection, graph/acceptance review, failure-matrix review and whitespace/diff checks. No executable red/green lane or test count is applicable. It establishes constraints and the model's unresolved inputs; it neither validates a model that does not exist nor proves runtime recovery.

Before the implementation obligation can be met, the separately modeled contract must be accepted and validated; every matrix family must execute against a frozen integrated implementation with retained failures, restart evidence and unchanged controls; package formatting/tests/strict Clippy and the full repository gates must pass.
